# Historical pre-release license review — 3.0.2

Archived evidence for the retired 3.0.2 release. The publication-hold status
below applies to that release and its original inputs. The current 3.3.1
restoration and component treatment are documented in [LICENSE_AUDIT.md](../LICENSE_AUDIT.md).

Review date: **12 November 2026**. Reviewed release: **3.0.2**.

**Status: publication is on hold pending the findings below.**

This is a practical review of the supplied files and public licensing evidence,
not a legal opinion or a guarantee that third-party rights have been cleared.
Missing evidence is distinguished from a proven prohibition. No rights holder
has been contacted, no permissions have been invented, and no new license has
been applied to someone else's work.

## Findings requiring action

### 1. The single NonCommercial license statement is not a sufficient license map

The root `LICENSE`, copied from the anatomy upstream, says the most restrictive
conditions propagate to the whole work and therefore labels the distribution
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. `NOTICE`, package metadata and the atlas information panel repeat
that conclusion. This should not be treated as clearance for every component.

The same project contains Z-Anatomy/BodyParts3D ShareAlike material, Wikipedia
text and NC components. CC BY-NC-SA is not a compatible adapter's license for
CC BY-SA material. Whether a particular combination is a collection of separate
works or an adaptation matters; simply choosing the most restrictive label
does not answer that question. This does not mean all mixed-license collections
are forbidden, nor does it automatically make an independent reader application
a derivative of every model it displays.

**Required:** document the applicable terms for each component and the rights
in the changes actually made. Resolve inseparable adaptations through compatible
replacement material or rights-holder permission. Preserve the upstream code's
own grant; do not casually relabel the entire project MIT or CC BY-SA either.

Sources: [CC's compatibility list](https://creativecommons.org/compatible-licenses/),
[CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en),
[Z-Anatomy upstream](https://github.com/Z-Anatomy/Models-of-human-anatomy),
[the anatomy viewer upstream](https://github.com/nqwrc/3d-anatomy).

### 2. Model provenance and the brain credit need clarification

The bundled original `models/License.txt` identifies the inner ear as
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and the kidney as CC BY-NC 4.0. Those restrictions must be
respected where the corresponding geometry is present. They cannot be removed
by changing the application's top-level license.

The original notice credits “Brainder” and “White matter” to the University of
Washington without specifying a license. A license does exist for Anderson
Winkler's **Brain for Blender** models: CC BY-SA 3.0. However, matching that grant
to every affected mesh in this exact export and correcting the existing credit
still needs a defensible provenance record. Our `nervous.glb` contains brain,
white-matter and ear structures, with materials including `Brain`, `Brain-Inner`
and `White matter`; names alone do not establish ownership of each mesh.

The project owner's public licensing document still lists the same reference
models and NC terms. Its current BodyParts3D credit differs from the old bundled
notice. Public upstream discussions also dispute exact kidney and brain origin.
These are evidence gaps, not a reason to silently discard original notices.

**Required:** identify the actual source/license for affected meshes, retain
correct creator credits and record any necessary permission. Replace or remove
uncleared geometry from every exported model and screenshot if it cannot be
cleared. That would be a product/content change and has not been done in this
review. A free GitHub download alone does not resolve these requirements.

Sources: [Brain for Blender, including its license](https://brainder.org/research/brain-for-blender/),
[upstream brain-license question and owner's response](https://github.com/Z-Anatomy/Models-of-human-anatomy/issues/10),
[disputed brain/white-matter credit](https://github.com/Z-Anatomy/Models-of-human-anatomy/issues/7),
[kidney provenance discussion](https://github.com/Z-Anatomy/Models-of-human-anatomy/issues/5),
[owner-linked licensing document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1peWW_7IiVgTTAwcI_auv38YSJ6qGWRuTMxzzhkRpXIk/edit).

### 3. Wikipedia-derived definitions lack adequate provenance

`definitions.json` contains **1,277 entries**. Only **187** include an explicit
Wikipedia article URL in their text; **1,090** do not. This is a provenance flag,
not proof that every one of those 1,090 entries is copied Wikipedia content.

The exporter truncates definitions to about 600 characters without preserving
source URLs in a separate field. The UI appends a Wikipedia **search** link,
which does not reliably identify the article whose text was reused. A generic
credit to Wikipedia at the project level does not establish authorship for all
of these passages. The historical CC BY-SA 3.0/GFDL statement also needs to be
matched to the actual source revision; current Wikipedia reuse guidance uses
CC BY-SA 4.0.

**Required:** recover real source article URLs, the applicable license and an
indication that extracts were shortened/edited. Use article links/history or
another valid author-attribution method. Do not guess article URLs from labels.
If source information cannot be recovered, replace the affected text with
verified original summaries or omit it until cleared.

Source: [Wikipedia's reuse requirements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reusing_Wikipedia_content).
See `license-audit/definitions-without-source.csv` for the review queue.

### 4. NIST spectral-table redistribution is not yet cleared

`content/data/spectra.json` contains **11,966 lines for 99 elements**, identified
as a selection from the 2005 Handbook of Basic Atomic Spectroscopic Data.
NIST identifies that handbook as **Standard Reference Database 108**. Its public
policy distinguishes ordinary government works from copyrighted SRD products.

The supplied project does not include a specific SRD 108 redistribution grant
or permission record. Do not assume every NIST dataset is public domain merely
because NIST is a US agency or the table can be downloaded without payment.
This review does not decide whether a particular numerical fact is protected;
the substantial compiled table and applicable database rights need separate
consideration.

**Required:** establish the specific terms authorizing this redistribution or
obtain permission; otherwise replace the compiled data with a cleared source.
Include the appropriate database citation and any required notice.

Sources: [NIST Handbook / SRD 108](https://www.nist.gov/pml/handbook-basic-atomic-spectroscopic-data),
[NIST copyright policy](https://www.nist.gov/open/license),
[NIST SRD public-law page](https://www.nist.gov/srd/public-law).

The [DOI registry metadata](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.18434/T4FW23)
was also checked: its rights entry points to the general NIST licensing page,
rather than naming a dataset-specific open license. That did not resolve the
redistribution question.

### 5. Draco license copy was missing; unpacked copies have been repaired

The project redistributed Draco JS/WASM decoders and mentioned Apache-2.0 in
`NOTICE`, but no full Draco license copy was included in its license directory.
The official upstream license has now been added as `draco-LICENSE` to:

- `content/licenses` and `content/anatomy/licenses`;
- `anatomy-source/public/licenses`, so an atlas rebuild retains it;
- the unpacked browser release's corresponding license directories.

The existing three.js, three-mesh-bvh and Inter notices are also preserved in
the atlas license directory. This fixes a documentation omission in source and
the unpacked browser edition; it does **not** settle the model/data findings.

**The existing Windows and Linux 3.0.2 installer binaries have not been rebuilt**
with these added notices. They must be regenerated and rechecked after the
remaining issues are resolved. Their checksums remain unchanged by this review.

Sources: [Draco license](https://github.com/google/draco/blob/main/LICENSE),
[Apache-2.0 redistribution conditions](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0).

### 6. Binary-runtime and installer obligations need a final release pass

Electron's MIT notice and `LICENSES.chromium.html` are present in the desktop
build payloads. Both the Debian archive and the compressed application payload
inside the Windows NSIS installer were inspected directly. Chromium's notice
includes FFmpeg's LGPL material and many other components; Electron's MIT
license alone is not a substitute for their requirements.

The Windows installer contains `StdUtils.dll`, `nsis7z.dll`, `UAC.dll`,
`System.dll`, `nsDialogs.dll`, `nsExec.dll` and `WinShell.dll`. A final plugin
license/source inventory must account for these binaries separately from the
installed Electron application. The supplied StdUtils include identifies
LGPL-2.1-or-later and the upstream clarification permits an independent installer
license when the unmodified DLL is used through the plugin interface. That does
not remove the library's own notice/source requirements. No completed record
of exact-binary source availability for these components is supplied here.

**Required:** document how LGPL components' corresponding-source and replacement
requirements are met for the exact distributed binaries, retain installer-plugin
notices, and verify the final installer payloads. Do not create an unapproved
multi-year source offer on the publisher's behalf.

Sources: [StdUtils license and clarification](https://github.com/lordmulder/StdUtils),
[NSIS license information](https://nsis.sourceforge.io/License).

## Release decision and next steps

1. Resolve component licenses and mesh provenance before public upload.
2. Restore definition-source attribution or replace uncleared extracts.
3. Resolve SRD 108 redistribution terms or replace the spectrum dataset.
4. Complete native/plugin notices and corresponding-source arrangements.
5. Rebuild the browser release and both installers; recheck their actual payloads
   and replace release checksums. Retain notices and state the changes made.

The review did not delete models, remove features or relicense upstream code.
It also did not send messages to any upstream authors. A local package that
runs correctly is not, by itself, evidence of permission to redistribute it.
